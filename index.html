Building a pool in Darwin

In Darwin a pool is used for much of the year, but the climate shapes the job. The Wet season is very humid and can slow excavation and outdoor work, so many people start the conversation in the Dry. Structures have to be designed for tropical cyclones, and shade, a cover and salt-air fittings matter on foreshore streets such as Nightcliff and Fannie Bay.

A high water table in the Wet, laterite or rocky ground, and termites are common site issues. Some foreshore streets also have coastal or storm-surge mapping. A local builder will look at all of this, along with the Territory's pool safety certificate rules, before quoting.

How much does a pool cost in Darwin?

Do I need approval for a new pool?

A building permit is not required for the pool itself, but on a property smaller than 1.8 hectares you must get an interim compliance certificate before construction and a compliance certificate before filling the pool to 30 cm or more. See our NT pool approvals guide.

Do I have to register the pool?

For a new pool on a property smaller than 1.8 hectares, you need a compliance certificate from the Swimming Pool Safety Authority. That certificate is the Territory's formal record that the barrier meets the Modified Australian Standard.

Do you cover Nightcliff, Fannie Bay, Rapid Creek, Muirhead, Leanyer, Parap, Stuart Park and Karama?

Yes. We take requests from across Darwin and nearby areas. The builder confirms whether they can service your street.

What's the difference between concrete, fibreglass and plunge pools?

Concrete is built on site and can be custom shaped. Fibreglass is a pre-formed shell that is delivered and installed. A plunge pool is a smaller pool in either material. See our pool types guide.
