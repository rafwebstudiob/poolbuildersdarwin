NT pool fencing and approvals in Darwin

In short: A new pool on a residential property smaller than 1.8 hectares in the Northern Territory needs an interim compliance certificate before construction, then a certified safety barrier and a compliance certificate before the pool is filled to 30 cm or more. A building permit is not required for the pool itself.

A summary of official rules, with links to the source pages. It is not legal advice.

Last updated: 24 November 2026

This page summarises official Northern Territory Government information so you know what to expect. It is not legal advice and doesn't replace a pool safety adviser at the Pool Fencing Unit. Follow the links and check with the Unit or your builder if you're unsure.

The short answer

If you own a residential property of less than 1.8 hectares and you want to install a pool or spa, you must also install a pool safety fence or barrier. Before the pool can be filled with more than 30 cm of water, the barrier must be installed to comply with the Modified Australian Standard, inspected by a pool safety adviser, and a compliance certificate must be issued by the Swimming Pool Safety Authority. NT Government says a building permit is not required for swimming pools, though a development permit may still be needed if planning rules require one.

Sources: NT Government, Construct or install a swimming pool or spa; NT Government, Work that requires permits.

Certificates before and after construction

Before construction begins, the owner must apply for an interim compliance certificate and give a copy to the pool contractor. After construction, you must get a compliance certificate within seven days of the end of construction and before the pool is filled to a depth of 30 cm or more. On properties of 1.8 hectares or larger you should still fence the pool, but a compliance certificate is not required unless you opt in.

Source: NT Government, Construct or install a swimming pool or spa.

The barrier standard

The laws are in the Swimming Pool Safety Act 2004. For a new pool on a small property (less than 1.8 hectares), the barrier must meet the Modified Australian Standard, which is based on AS 1926.1 (1993) and AS 1926.2 (1995) with Territory modifications, including how dividing fences are measured. The Pool Fencing Unit can explain how to meet the standard.

Source: NT Government, Pool fencing and barrier safety standards.

Local council: Darwin

Your local council is City of Darwin. Check the council's planning information for your address (zoning, overlays, bush fire, flood or heritage mapping), as these can change what is allowed for a pool. Your builder or certifier normally does this check as part of the quote.

Darwin is in a tropical cyclone region, so structures have to be designed for that. Some foreshore streets also have coastal or storm-surge mapping, and termites are a common building consideration in the Top End. A builder or certifier will check this for your block.

Builders and contracts

A building permit is not required for the pool itself, so the usual prescribed residential building work contract and fidelity fund rules that apply to a new house may not apply to a pool-only job. You should still use a written contract and check who is responsible for the interim certificate, the barrier and the compliance certificate. Plumbing and electrical work must be done by licensed practitioners.

In the Northern Territory, prescribed residential building work must be done by a building contractor registered with the Building Practitioners Board, unless you become a registered owner-builder. That includes a new home, and renovations or extensions that are prescribed (including work worth more than $25,000 that increases the residential floor area). A residential building contract is required for prescribed work; under the standard progress-payment schedule the deposit before work starts can be no more than 5% of the contract price. For prescribed work, the builder must also obtain a fidelity fund certificate (the Territory's residential building cover) before a building permit is granted and before demanding payment. You can check a practitioner on the Building Practitioners Board register.

Sources: NT Government, Residential building work that requires a registered builder; NT Government, Signing a residential building contract; NT Government, Fidelity fund certificate; Building Practitioners Board.
